\begin{abstract}
  Grokking is delayed generalisation of a network, but what determines
  the delay is disputed. We decompose the movement of the neural tangent
  kernel into its scale and its shape, measuring both on a two-layer network
  learning modular addition across laziness, width and weight decay. At a
  fixed width, the shape change is the statistic most tied to the grokking
  step, even though some networks reach it without grokking. The grokking
  time grows with width and falls with weight decay, which neither our nor the literature hypotheses predict alone. 
  Shape is the
  best single early predictor of the grokking time. We also measure the same
  statistics on MNIST, where grokking is induced differently by
  scaling up the initial weights. On this task the weight-decay clock\footnote{We define a clock to be a measurement we can use to predict the grokking time} holds
  and width drops out.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Grokking is the delayed generalisation that
\citet{powerGrokkingGeneralizationOverfitting2022} found on small algorithmic
datasets. A network fits its training data early, and its test
accuracy rises long
afterwards. Modular addition learned by a two-layer perceptron
is a small instance of it. Full-batch gradient descent on the squared error
groks there with no explicit regularisation
\citep{gromovGrokkingModularArithmetic2023}. What sets the length of the delay
is an open question.

To answer this question, we look to the neural tangent kernel, a matrix derived from the network that captures how the network perceives the similarity between a set of data points. We discuss it in more depth in \ref{sec:methods}.

% \cite{jacot2018ntk} defines the Neural Tangent Kernel to relate the
% behaviour of neural networks to kernel ridge regression.

One account holds that grokking is a transition from lazy training to rich
training \citep{kumarGrokkingTransitionLazy2024}. In lazy training the network
behaves as a kernel method with its initial tangent kernel
\citep{chizatLazyTrainingDifferentiable2020}. In rich training the
kernel adapts to the task. The evidence that the kernel changes is a distance:
the empirical tangent kernel stays close to its initial value until the training
data are fitted, and moves far from it afterwards
\citep{mohamadiWhyYouGrok2024}. A distance does not say how the kernel changed.
A kernel can grow in scale while its eigenvectors are unchanged. It can also
change its shape, so that its eigenvectors change.

The difference matters for the accounts of the grokking time. Recent work times
grokking by scale-like parameters: the weight norm
\citep{khanhWeightNormSets2026}, the scale of the
logits \citep{khanhWhatDoesWeight2026}, or the weight-decay rate
\citep{kimGrokkingWeightDecayClock2026,pracherSpectralTheoryGrokking2026}. The
result that \citet{kumarGrokkingTransitionLazy2024} cite for weight
decay forcing
the kernel to change is that of \citet{lewkowyczTrainingDynamicsDeep2021}, in
which the kernel only shrinks. None of these accounts measures whether the
kernel changes shape.

Knowing when a network will grok has real applications. The tangent
kernel can be measured on any network trained by gradient descent, while other predictors are not always known. An estimate of the
time lets training stop once a network has generalised, saving compute resources.
It also reveals when, during training, a capability appears, which is useful when auditing or understanding a model. As such, being able to predict the grokking from the tangent kernel has broader implications when it comes to the resource efficiency of training models and the understanding of the models themselves. This is especially applicable for models that are more capable and compute intensive to train. 

We ask what sets the grokking time: the shape of the kernel, its scale, or the
weight-decay rate. Our hypothesis is that the shape change sets it.
We measure three
statistics of the empirical tangent kernel along training: its scale $S_t$,
shape $R_t$ and alignment $A_t$ with the labels. We do so on a grid of
720 runs that crosses laziness, width and weight decay. Our contributions are as
follows.
\begin{enumerate}[1.]
  \item We split the movement of the kernel into a change of scale and a
    change of shape, using each one as a timing variable. On the baseline of
    \citet{kumarGrokkingTransitionLazy2024}, the usual distance is mostly a
    change of scale: at $\alpha=1$ the scale accounts for
    \NtwoAOneGrok{} of $D_t^2$
    at grokking.
  \item We test whether grokking happens at a fixed value of each statistic.
    We use a placebo test that reads each run at another run's grokking step to distinguish between a statistic that merely stays in a narrow band and a genuine threshold.
  \item We fit a power law for how the grokking time scales
    with model width and weight decay. The hypotheses that the shape change
    sets the grokking time and that weight decay sets it predict different
    exponents.
  \item We test whether the statistics can be used to predict
    grokking ahead of time.
  \item We repeat the first two tests on the MNIST dataset, a task on which
    \citet{liuOmnigrokGrokkingAlgorithmic2023} induce grokking
    through a different method,
    to test whether our findings generalise.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Grokking.}
\citet{powerGrokkingGeneralizationOverfitting2022} report grokking on small
algorithmic datasets. \citet{nandaProgressMeasuresGrokking2023} reverse-engineer
the algorithm a small transformer learns for modular addition and split training
into memorisation, circuit formation and clean-up.
\citet{gromovGrokkingModularArithmetic2023} gives closed-form weights that solve
modular addition with a quadratic activation, and observes grokking under
full-batch gradient descent with no explicit regularisation.

\paragraph{Lazy and rich training.}
In the infinite-width limit the tangent kernel is constant, so the network
trains as a kernel method \citep{jacotNeuralTangentKernel2020}.
\citet{chizatLazyTrainingDifferentiable2020} show that lazy training is a matter
of scale. Multiplying a model's output by a factor $\alpha$ brings its training
closer to that of its linearisation as $\alpha$ grows.
\citet{kumarGrokkingTransitionLazy2024} use this factor to move a network
between the two regimes, and propose that grokking is the transition from lazy
to rich dynamics. \citet{mohamadiWhyYouGrok2024} prove that a
permutation-equivariant kernel method needs a constant fraction of the $p^2$
pairs to learn modular addition, and observe that the kernel moves far from its
initial value only after the training data are fitted.

\paragraph{What sets the grokking time.}
\citet{khanhWeightNormSets2026} find that the weight norm sets the timescale of
grokking. A network whose norm is clamped at $\rho W_c$, with $W_c$ the norm
at the grokking step, groks at whatever norm it is held at, after a time that
grows exponentially in $\rho$.
\citet{khanhWhatDoesWeight2026} show that under cross-entropy the norm acts
through the scale of the logits, and that under squared error it acts through a
different route. \citet[Cor.~5, Eq.~20, arXiv
v1]{kimGrokkingWeightDecayClock2026}
derives, for linear models trained with heavy-ball momentum $\beta$ and weight
decay, a grokking time of about $(1-\beta)/(\eta\lambda)$ times a
logarithm of an
initial amplitude, and observes this scaling on modular addition.
\citet{pracherSpectralTheoryGrokking2026} predict, for homogeneous networks
trained on the squared error with weight decay, that the grokking time is set by
the product of the learning rate and the weight decay.
\citet{liuOmnigrokGrokkingAlgorithmic2023}
describe the same relationship in terms of the weight norm.
When weight decay is the only regulariser and the training loss is near zero
after the data are fitted, a network starting with norm $w_0$ above a critical
value $w_c$ generalises after roughly $\ln(w_0/w_c)/(\eta\lambda)$ steps.
They induce grokking on MNIST by increasing $w_0$.

\paragraph{The kernel under weight decay.}
We write $\partial_\mu$ for the derivative with respect to the parameter
$\theta_\mu$, so that the empirical tangent kernel is
$\Theta_t(x,x') = \sum_\mu \partial_\mu f_t(x)\,\partial_\mu f_t(x')$.
A network is homogeneous of degree $k$ if $f(c\theta;x) = c^k f(\theta;x)$
for all $c > 0$. Consider such a network trained by gradient flow with regularisation
$\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\lVert\theta\rVert^2$. Its kernel evolves as
\begin{align}
  \frac{d\Theta_t(x,x')}{dt} &= -2(k-1)\lambda\,\Theta_t(x,x')
  + T_t(x,x') + T_t(x',x),
  \label{eq:kernelflow}\\
  T_t(x,x') &= -\sum_{x''}\sum_{\mu,\nu}\partial_\mu\partial_\nu f_t(x)\,
  \partial_\mu f_t(x')\,\partial_\nu f_t(x'')\,\ell'(x''),\nonumber
\end{align}
where $x''$ ranges over the training inputs and $\ell'(x'')$ is the derivative
of the loss with respect to the output at $x''$
\citep[Eqs.~S5 and S6, arXiv v2]{lewkowyczTrainingDynamicsDeep2021}.
Weight decay appears only in the first term, which multiplies the kernel by a
scalar and leaves its eigenvectors fixed. Only $T_t$, which involves the second
derivatives of $f$ with respect to the parameters, can change the kernel's
shape. At initialisation $T_0$ has expectation of order $1/N$ in the width $N$
\citep{lewkowyczTrainingDynamicsDeep2021}.

\paragraph{Predictions.}
\Cref{tab:predictions} sets out what each account predicts for the three tests
of this report. If the shape change sets the grokking
time and $T_t$ stays of order $1/N$ along
training, a wider network changes shape more slowly and groks later
at a fixed weight
decay.
The weight decay should then matter little. A weight-decay
clock predicts % do we need to define what we mean by clock somewhere?
the opposite. The term weight-decay \textit{clock} refers to weight-decay setting grokking time.
Each account was tested on its own. The weight-decay accounts also leave

\section{Methods}\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Task, network and training}\label{sec:setup}

The task we train on is modular addition with modulus $p=23$, the baseline of
\citet[App.~8.3, arXiv v3]{kumarGrokkingTransitionLazy2024}. Each
pair $(a,b)$ of
residues is encoded as the concatenation $x_{a,b}\in\mathbb{R}^{2p}$ of the
one-hot codes of $a$ and $b$. Its target $y_{a,b}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}$ is the
one-hot code of $(a+b)\bmod p$. The dataset holds all $p^2=529$ pairs. Each seed
draws its own split, with 476 pairs for training and 53 for testing, and its own
initial weights.

The network is a two-layer perceptron without biases,
\begin{equation}
  f(x;\theta) = N^{-1/2}\,W^{(2)}\phi\bigl(D^{-1/2}W^{(1)}x\bigr),
  \label{eq:network}
\end{equation}
with input dimension $D=2p$, width $N$, weights $W^{(1)}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times
D}$ and $W^{(2)}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times N}$ drawn from
$\mathcal{N}(0,1)$, and the
rectifier $\phi(h)=\max\{h,0\}$. The factor $N^{-1/2}$ is the NTK
parameterisation of \citet{jacotNeuralTangentKernel2020}. Following
\citet{kumarGrokkingTransitionLazy2024}, the trained predictor is
$\tilde f(x;\theta)=\alpha[f(x;\theta)-f(x;\theta_0)]$ with learning rate
$\eta=\eta_0/\alpha^2$. A larger $\alpha$ gives lazier training. The loss is the
squared error averaged over the outputs and the training pairs. Training is
full-batch gradient descent with weight decay,
$\theta_{k+1}=(1-\eta\lambda)\theta_k-\eta\nabla L(\theta_k)$. For plain
gradient descent this is the same as $L_2$ regularisation
\citep{kimGrokkingWeightDecayClock2026}. \Cref{tab:hyper}
(Appendix~\ref{app:settings}) lists every setting. The code that trains the
runs and rebuilds every figure, table and number is in \file{report_v2/code/}.

\subsection{The grid}\label{sec:grid}

Each axis of the grid tests one account. The output scale $\alpha$ sets how lazy
the training is \citep{chizatLazyTrainingDifferentiable2020}. The width $N$ sets
the size of $T_t$ in~\eqref{eq:kernelflow}, and so how fast the kernel can
change shape. The weight decay $\eta\lambda$ sets the clock of
\citet{kimGrokkingWeightDecayClock2026}. We call one combination of
$(\alpha,N,\eta\lambda)$ a \emph{cell}. The five runs of a cell share every
setting except for the seed, which sets the split and the initial
weights. The grid has $3\times6\times8=144$ cells and 720 runs, and every run
finished.

\subsection{Scale, shape and alignment}\label{sec:statistics}

We measure the tangent kernel of one scalar output, the sum of the logits
divided by $\sqrt p$, $g(x;\theta)=p^{-1/2}\sum_{c}f_c(x;\theta)$. This is the
pseudo-NTK of \citet{mohamadiFastWellFoundedApproximation2022}. On the 53 test
pairs of a seed, the kernel at step $t$ is the matrix with entries
$[K_t]_{ij}=\langle\nabla_\theta g(x_i;\theta_t),\nabla_\theta
g(x_j;\theta_t)\rangle$.
The kernel of $\tilde f$ is $\alpha^2$ times the kernel of $f$, so the ratios
below do not depend on which of the two is used. With $Y$ the one-hot targets
of the test pairs and $C=I-\frac1{53}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top$, we record
\begin{equation}
  S_t = \frac{\Fnorm{K_t}}{\Fnorm{K_0}},\qquad
  R_t = 1-\frac{\langle K_t,K_0\rangle_F}{\Fnorm{K_t}\,\Fnorm{K_0}},\qquad
  A_t = \frac{\langle CK_tC,\,CYY^\top C\rangle_F}
  {\Fnorm{CK_tC}\,\Fnorm{CYY^\top C}}.
  \label{eq:statistics}
\end{equation}
The scale $S_t$ is 1 at initialisation, and a value above 1 means the kernel
grew. The shape $R_t$ measures how far the kernel has changed shape, and it is
the kernel distance of \citet{fortDeepLearningKernel2020}. It is 0 at
initialisation and does not change when the kernel is rescaled. The
alignment $A_t$ is the centred
alignment of \citet{cortesAlgorithmsLearningKernels2024} with the label kernel
$YY^\top$, and it also does not change when the kernel is rescaled.

The usual measure of kernel movement is the relative variation
$D_t=\Fnorm{K_t-K_0}/\Fnorm{K_0}$ \citep{geigerDisentanglingFeatureLazy2020}. It
is a function of the other two,
\begin{equation}
  D_t^2 = S_t^2 - 2S_t(1-R_t) + 1,
  \label{eq:identity}
\end{equation}
(\cref{app:identity}). A kernel that only grows has $R_t=0$ and
$D_t=\lvert S_t-1\rvert$. A large $D_t$ does not show that the kernel
changed shape.

\subsection{Event times}\label{sec:events}

The memorisation step of a run is the first checkpoint at which its training
accuracy reaches 0.99. Its grokking step $T$ is the first checkpoint at which
its test accuracy reaches 0.95. Appendix~\ref{app:levels} repeats the analysis
at the levels 0.80 and 0.90. Both events are counted from step 0. A run that
does not reach a level within 200\,000 steps is kept, and its grokking step is
recorded as censored at the budget (200\,000 steps). The events are
read on the checkpoint grid described above,
so each one is known to lie between two checkpoints. The gap to the
checkpoint before is at most \NthreeGrok{} of the grokking step and
\NthreeMem{} of the memorisation step.
% We use accuracy rather than loss because
% the test loss levels off above zero after the test accuracy is perfect. In the

\subsection{Analysis}\label{sec:analysis}

\paragraph{Thresholds (Tier 1).} For each statistic we take its value at the
grokking step of every run that groks, and measure the spread across runs by the
standard deviation (SD). A statistic can have a small spread because it stays in
a narrow band all through training, so we include a placebo test to
separate the two cases.
The placebo test reads each run at the grokking step of another run,
drawn by a random
permutation with no fixed points, and measures the SD again. The SD
ratio is the median placebo SD over 2\,000 permutations divided by the SD at the
true events, and $p$ is the share of permutations whose SD is no larger. A
ratio near 1 means the statistic is not tied to the event. The SD has the
units of its statistic, so the SDs of different statistics cannot be compared
directly.

\paragraph{Scaling (Tier 2).} We fit the log-normal accelerated failure time
model
\begin{equation}
  \log T = c + a\log N + b\log(\eta\lambda) + \gamma\,\log N\,\log(\eta\lambda)
  + \sigma\varepsilon,\qquad \varepsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1),
  \label{eq:aft}
\end{equation}
by maximum likelihood. A censored run contributes the probability that its
grokking step exceeds the budget
\citep{kalbfleischStatisticalAnalysisFailure2002}. Both logarithms
are centred at
the middle of the grid, so $a$ and $b$ are the slopes there. The fits use
lifelines \citep{davidson-pilonLifelinesSurvivalAnalysis2019}. The intervals
resample whole cells and then seeds within cells, so that they include the
misfit of the cells about the fitted plane.

\paragraph{Prediction (Tier 3).} We read $S_t$, $\log R_t$ and $A_t$ at
$\tstar=2\,430$, a saved step before any run reaches the grokking level, and
ask how well they predict the grokking step. At $\tstar$ the values of $R_t$
cluster near zero. \EtenRawRSmall{} of the runs that grok have $R_t$ below
0.01, and the largest is \EtenRawRMax. Taking the logarithm spreads them out
and predicts better, so we use $\log R_t$. Kernel ridge on all three statistics
reaches \EtenRawKrrSRARsq{} with $R_t$ and \EtenSRARsq{} with $\log R_t$.
The five seeds of a cell are close
copies, so whole groups of runs are always held out. Two tests are used. The
first holds out one cell at a time, which tests interpolation. Kernel ridge
regression with a Gaussian kernel is fitted on each subset of the statistics,
which shows which statistics contain the information. The second holds out one
value of $\alpha$ at a time, which tests extrapolation to an unseen laziness.
It compares a linear accelerated failure time model on each statistic alone,
gradient-boosted trees with the accelerated failure time objective
\citep{chenXGBoostScalableTree2016} on all three, and a gated recurrent unit
\citep{choLearningPhraseRepresentations2014} that reads all three at the 25
saved steps up to $\tstar$. The weight decay does not enter as a predictor,
since it is a setting and not a measurement (\cref{sec:tier3}). As a reference,
the second test also fits the trees to the settings $N$ and $\eta\lambda$
together and to each one alone. Two scores are used. $R^2$ is the share of the
spread in $\log T$ explained on held-out runs. The concordance index
is the share of usable pairs of runs, those whose order censoring does not
hide, that a model puts in the right order. A value of 0.5 indicates no
predictive discrimination and 1.0 perfect separation
\citep{harrellEvaluatingYieldMedical1982,harrellMULTIVARIABLEPROGNOSTICMODELS1996}.
Appendix~\ref{app:fitting} gives the
settings of each model.

\subsection{The second task (Tier 4)}\label{sec:mnistsetup}

In Tier 4, we apply Tiers 1 and 2 to the MNIST dataset, in the setup with
which \citet{liuOmnigrokGrokkingAlgorithmic2023} induce grokking. The network is
trained on 1\,000 images, and its initial weights are multiplied by a scale
$s>1$, so weight decay must shrink the norm before the network generalises.
\Cref{sec:setup} instead subtracts $f(x;\theta_0)$, so that every run starts
from the zero function. On MNIST this centring removes the delay, since the
training and test accuracies then rise together, so we do not use it.
The architecture is a bias-free three-layer rectifier network of width $N$
in the standard parameterisation, trained by minibatch gradient
descent without momentum,
with the learning rate reset from the curvature every $10000$ steps.
The values tested on the grid are $s\in\{3,5,7,9\}$,
$N\in\{100,200,400,800\}$ and
seven decays from 0 to $4\times10^{-5}$, each with 5 seeds.
Each run has a budget of $500000$ steps and the grokking level is $0.80$
since no run reaches 0.95.

\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Baseline: the reproduction of Kumar et al.}\label{sec:baseline}

\textbf{Finding.} Our runs reproduce the baseline of
\citet{kumarGrokkingTransitionLazy2024}. The network memorises within a few
thousand steps and groks much later, with lazier networks grokking even later. Over this
time the kernel grows several-fold while $R_t$ stays below 0.09.
% 0.09 bounds the largest R_sum over every checkpoint of the 15 baseline runs
% in code/data/checkpoints_slim.parquet (0.086, at alpha = 0.5).

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.92\linewidth]{figures/E1_baseline.pdf}
  \caption{Training at the baseline of \citet{kumarGrokkingTransitionLazy2024}
    ($N=100$, $\eta\lambda=0$) for three values of $\alpha$. Each line is the
    median over 5 seeds and the shading is their range. Darker lines are lazier
    networks. A hollow marker is the median memorisation step and a filled
    marker the median grokking step. No seed groks at $\alpha=2$. $R_t$ is on a
  log scale.}
  \label{fig:baseline}
\end{figure}

At $\alpha=0.5$ and $\alpha=1$ every seed memorises and then groks, with median
grokking steps of \NfiveAHalfGrokMedian{} and \NfiveAOneGrokMedian{}
(\cref{fig:baseline}). At $\alpha=2$ no seed groks within 200\,000 steps. These
grokking steps and their order in $\alpha$ are close to those of
\citet[Fig.~2(c), arXiv v3]{kumarGrokkingTransitionLazy2024}.
Appendix~\ref{app:kumar} compares the settings and the event steps one by one.

By~\eqref{eq:identity} the squared variation splits into a scale part
$(S_t-1)^2$ and a shape part $2S_tR_t$. At $\alpha=1$ the scale part is
\NtwoAOneGrok{} of $D_t^2$ at grokking. The distance $D_t$ mostly records
the growth of the kernel, so we measure $S_t$ and $R_t$ separately.

\subsection{Tier 1: is there a threshold?}\label{sec:tier1}

\textbf{Finding.} At a fixed width, shape is the statistic most tied to the
grokking step, but reaching that shape change does not make a network grok. The
alignment at grokking barely differs from its value at initialisation. Across
widths, no statistic takes a fixed value at grokking.

given the same placebo check.

\subsection{Tier 2: width or weight decay?}\label{sec:tier2}

\textbf{Finding.} The grokking time depends on both width and weight decay, so
neither the shape clock nor the weight-decay clock of
\cref{tab:predictions} holds on its own. Width sets how much the kernel changes
shape. At a fixed width, weight decay shrinks its scale.

\subsection{Tier 3: predicting the grokking time from the
kernel}\label{sec:tier3}

\textbf{Finding.} Read before any run groks, shape is the best single predictor
of the grokking time on held-out cells. Scale adds information that
shape lacks, and alignment
adds little. On an unseen $\alpha$, the three statistics together rank runs
better than any one of them alone. The settings $N$ and $\eta\lambda$ rank them
better still, and better than either setting alone, which supports the mixed
clock of \cref{sec:tier2}.

Each account in \cref{tab:predictions} names a different early predictor:
$R_t$ for the shape clock, $S_t$ for the norm or scale clock, and
$\eta\lambda$ alone for the weight-decay clock. This tier tests the first
two, with $A_t$ beside them. It does not test $\eta\lambda$, which we set and
know exactly. We want
predictors that carry over to real models, so this tier measures how much of the
grokking time the kernel statistics recover on their own. A model given the
settings $N$ and $\eta\lambda$ is a reference.

enlarged initial function must decay, and the kernel need only shrink

modular addition the delay happens because of the centring. It is not surprising that rotation is not required for the MNIST task, as the data is largely linearly separable, not requiring a unique kernel to be learnt.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

Every modular addition run uses the NTK parameterisation (Section \ref{sec:setup}), one task and
one architecture. The measured width effects therefore depend on this
parameterisation. Each cell has 5 seeds. The events are known only to the
resolution of the checkpoint grid. The fits in Tier 2 depend on which cells
enter, and the decay edge bends the plane (\cref{tab:scaling}). The kernel
statistics say nothing about which seed of a cell groks first, and the
reference model given the settings predicts better than any kernel model. All
three statistics use the kernel of the summed logits.
\citet{mohamadiFastWellFoundedApproximation2022} find that this kernel
approximates the tangent kernel of all $p$ logits less well as training goes
on. The centred predictor is not homogeneous,
so the theorem behind~\eqref{eq:kernelflow} applies to our network only as a
guide. The proposal also planned a clamped-norm arm, a sweep over $p$ and the
training fraction, and a cross-entropy arm. They were not run. The proposal
defined the events by the loss. The report uses accuracy (\cref{sec:events}).

The MNIST runs differ in not only the task but the architecture and
method for inducing grokking. Our
comparison assumes that none of these changes
which clock applies. With only two tasks to compare we cannot tell
which of them,
if any, is responsible for the different result. Two small tasks can show that
a clock fails, as the width exponent does on MNIST, and they cannot fix the
exponents for other architectures, losses or scales. Tier 4 tests which clock
sets the time once a delay exists, not whether a shape change can cause one.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked what sets the grokking time: the shape of the tangent kernel, its
scale, or the weight-decay rate. On modular addition the answer is a mixed
clock. Shape is the most informative of the three statistics. At a fixed width
it is the most tied to the grokking step, and read early it is the best single
predictor of the time. It is not enough on its own, and it is not a fixed
threshold. Networks can reach the shape change at which runs grok without
grokking, its value at grokking changes with width and $\alpha$, and early
scale adds information that shape lacks. The grokking time grows with
width and falls with weight
decay, so neither our shape clock nor the weight-decay clock holds alone. Width
sets the shape change, and at a fixed width weight decay shrinks the scale.
The two settings together also predict the time better than either one alone.
On MNIST, \citet{liuOmnigrokGrokkingAlgorithmic2023} create the delay by
enlarging the initial weights. There the time follows the law
\(\ln(s/n_c)/(\eta\lambda)\) of the weight-decay clock, and width sets the
shape change without setting the time. What is new is the
split into scale and shape, the placebo test,
the crossed grid on two tasks, and prediction from quantities that any network
exposes. A next step is to test whether the early rate of shape change falls as
$1/N$, and whether a kernel frozen at each step can generalise on either task.

\section{The identity for the variation}\label{app:identity}

Expanding the squared norm gives
$\Fnorm{K_t-K_0}^2=\Fnorm{K_t}^2-2\langle K_t,K_0\rangle_F+\Fnorm{K_0}^2$.
Dividing by $\Fnorm{K_0}^2$ and using
$\langle K_t,K_0\rangle_F=(1-R_t)\Fnorm{K_t}\Fnorm{K_0}$
from~\eqref{eq:statistics} gives $D_t^2=S_t^2-2S_t(1-R_t)+1$. By the
Cauchy-Schwarz
inequality, $R_t=0$ exactly when $K_t=cK_0$ for some $c>0$, and then
$D_t=\lvert S_t-1\rvert$.

\section{The baseline against Kumar et al.}\label{app:kumar}

\Cref{tab:kumar-settings} sets each setting of our baseline beside
\citet[arXiv v3]{kumarGrokkingTransitionLazy2024}, read from their Appendices
8.1 and 8.3, Section 3 and Figure 2, and \cref{tab:kumar-events} sets our event
steps beside the steps read by eye from their Figure 2. Their grokking at
$\alpha=0.5$ and $\alpha=1$ falls close to ours. Their training accuracy rises
much more slowly, reaching 100\% near 20\,000 epochs at $\alpha=1$, where our
runs reach 0.99 by about 3\,000 steps. They do not state the parameterisation
or the initialisation, which may explain this gap. The reproduction
matches on the grokking time and on the order in $\alpha$, and does not match
on the memorisation time.

\Cref{sec:baseline} gives the split of the variation at grokking for $\alpha=1$.
The scale part $(S_t-1)^2$ is \NtwoAOneMem{} of $D_t^2$ at memorisation and
\NtwoAOneFinal{} at the last step, where $S_t$ is \NtwoAOneSfinal{} and $R_t$
is only \NtwoAOneRfinal. At grokking it is \NtwoAHalfGrok{} for $\alpha=0.5$,
and for $\alpha=2$ it is \NtwoATwoMem{} at memorisation and \NtwoATwoFinal{}
at the last step.

\section{Settings of the prediction models}\label{app:fitting}

All models use the 630 runs with $\eta\lambda\le10^{-3}$, with $S_t$,
$\log R_t$ and $A_t$ read at $\tstar$. The grokking step of a censored run is
known only to exceed 200\,000. Whole groups are held out: one value of $\alpha$
at a time (\cref{fig:ladder}) or one cell at a time (\cref{tab:subsets}). The
file \file{report_v2/code/models.py} fixes every setting below.

\paragraph{Linear models.} The log-normal accelerated failure time model of a
single statistic standardises it on the training fold and uses a ridge penalty
of 0.01.

\paragraph{Kernel ridge.} The kernel is Gaussian on standardised inputs. Its
penalty in $\{0.01,0.1,1\}$ and its width in $\{0.1,0.5,2\}$ are
chosen by grouped
5-fold cross-validation over the training cells, inside every outer fold. It is
fitted to $\log T$ on the training runs that grok.

\paragraph{Gradient-boosted trees.} The trees use the accelerated failure time
objective with normal errors of scale 1 and a subsample of 0.8. The
search covers
depths 1 to 4, learning rates 0.03 and 0.1, and minimum child weights
1, 5 and 10,
with up to 1\,000 rounds and early stopping after 50. The setting with the best
concordance on 20\% of the training cells is refitted on all training runs. The
seeds are 0 to 4. The reference model is the same search on $\log N$ and
$\log_{10}(\eta\lambda)$, with $\eta\lambda=0$ set to $10^{-6}$. Its
two parts are
the same search on $\log N$ alone and on $\log_{10}(\eta\lambda)$ alone.

\paragraph{Recurrent network.} One gated recurrent layer with 16 units reads
$A_t$, $S_t$ and $R_t$ at the 25 saved steps up to $\tstar$. $R_t$ enters
without a logarithm because it is 0 at step 0. The inputs are standardised on
the training fold. A linear head gives the location and a learned parameter the
scale of a log-normal accelerated failure time loss. Training uses Adam with
learning rate 0.01 and weight decay $10^{-4}$, on the full batch, for up to 800
epochs. Every 10 epochs the loss on 20\% of the training cells is
checked, and the
best weights are kept. Early stopping chose between \EfifteenGruEpochsMin{} and
\EfifteenGruEpochsMax{} epochs. The seeds are 0 to 2.

\section{Sensitivity to the grokking level}\label{app:levels}

\Cref{tab:levels-thresholds} repeats the placebo test at the levels 0.80 and
0.90, and \cref{tab:levels-scaling} repeats the main fit. At $N=100$
the order of
the statistics is the same at every level. $R_t$ tightens most, with ratios of
\EsixteenEightyNhundredRRatio{} at 0.80 and \EsixteenNinetyNhundredRRatio{} at
0.90, and $S_t$ comes next. Across widths $R_t$ never passes. At 0.80 the
scale and the alignment tighten slightly across widths, with $p$ of
\EsixteenEightyAoneSP{} and \EsixteenEightyAoneAP{} and ratios of only
\EsixteenEightyAoneSRatio{} and \EsixteenEightyAoneARatio. At 0.90 only the
alignment does ($p=$ \EsixteenNinetyAoneAP); the scale has $p=$
\EsixteenNinetyAoneSP. The exponents $a$ and
$b$ keep their signs at every level, and both intervals exclude zero.

\section{Settings of the MNIST runs}\label{app:mnist}

\Cref{tab:mnist-settings} gives every setting of the MNIST sweep beside
the modular addition setting it replaces. \Cref{tab:mnist-rows} gives the
fit of \cref{sec:tier4mnist} with its check rows, and the exponents are the
same in every row. The runs of one width and one
scale were trained together as one fused model on one H100, in
\EnineteenHours{} summed GPU hours, with TF32 matrix multiplies. A second
sweep in full float32 gave the same grokking outcome in every one of the 560
runs and the same memorisation outcome in all but 3.
% The float32 comparison is Zac Kienzle's certification run of 19 October 2026
% (commit 42c6fbf, mnist_sweep.py certify): exact McNemar test, 0 flips at
% level 0.80 and 3 flips in memorisation.

Some outcomes stay out of \cref{sec:tier4mnist}. Width 100 reaches the
level in \EnineteenWhundredReached{} of its 120 runs with decay, but its test
accuracy at memorisation is already \EnineteenWhundredAccmem{}, so there is
little delay to time; it reaches the level later than the wider networks at
the same decay, with a lower and scattered norm, so it does not follow the
law and is left out of the fits. We read this as a limit of capacity at
1\,000 images. Without weight decay no run reaches the level within the
budget. A network at $s=3$ already has test accuracy
\EnineteenSthreeAccmem{} when it memorises. The norm
follows pure decay, $\lVert\theta_T\rVert=\lVert\theta_0\rVert
e^{-\eta\lambda T}$, to within \EnineteenPureGap{} of the initial norm, and
the law of \cref{sec:tier4mnist} has correlation \EnineteenCorr{} with the
measured product $\eta\lambda T$. The alignment at grokking is
\EnineteenAgrok{} against \EnineteenAzero{} at initialisation, as on modular
addition. Under the placebo every statistic tightens, because each is a
function of the step and the grokking steps span more than a decade: the
ratios are \EnineteenNtwohundredSRatio{} to \EnineteenNtwohundredARatio{} at
$N=200$ and \EnineteenScalesevenRRatio{} to \EnineteenScalesevenSRatio{}
across widths. Tier 3 is not repeated: with $\sigma$ of \EnineteenMainS{},
an early read of the norm predicts the time and the shape does not.
The centred pilot of
\cref{sec:mnistsetup} used the two-layer network of \cref{sec:setup} on
MNIST with initial weights times 3, full-batch gradient descent,
$\eta\lambda=10^{-5}$, one seed and 200\,000 steps, at
$\alpha\in\{1,0.5,0.05,0.01\}$. Every run memorised within 1\,500 steps with
test accuracy between 0.87 and 0.90, and the run at $\alpha=0.001$ did not
memorise.
