\begin{abstract}
  Grokking is delayed generalisation of a network, but what determines
  the delay is disputed. We decompose the movement of the neural tangent
  kernel into its scale and rotation, measuring both on a two-layer network
  learning modular addition across laziness, width and weight decay. At a
  fixed width, rotation is the statistic most tied to the grokking step, even
  though some networks reach it without grokking. The grokking time grows
  with width and falls with weight decay, which neither a rotation clock nor
  a weight-decay clock predicts alone. Read early, rotation is the best
  single predictor of the grokking time. We also measure the same statistics
  on MNIST, where grokking is induced in a different way by scaling up the
  initial weights. On this task the weight-decay clock holds and width drops
  out. Weight decay therefore sets the time through the weight norm on both
  tasks, and width enters only where the task forces the kernel to change
  shape.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\budget{1.0 page}

Grokking is the delayed generalisation that
\citet{powerGrokkingGeneralizationOverfitting2022} found on small algorithmic
datasets. A network fits its training data early, and its test accuracy rises
significantly long afterwards. Modular addition learned by a two-layer perceptron
is a small instance of it. Full-batch gradient descent on the squared error
groks there with no explicit regularisation
\citep{gromovGrokkingModularArithmetic2023}. What sets the length of the delay
is an open question.

% \cite{jacot2018ntk} defines the Neural Tangent Kernel to relate the behaviour of neural networks to kernel ridge regression.

One account holds that grokking is a transition from lazy training to rich
training \citep{kumarGrokkingTransitionLazy2024}. In lazy training the network
behaves as a kernel method with its initial tangent kernel. In rich training the
kernel adapts to the task. The evidence that the kernel changes is a distance:
the empirical tangent kernel stays close to its initial value until the training
data are fitted, and moves far from it afterwards
\citep{mohamadiWhyYouGrok2024}. A distance does not say how the kernel changed.
A kernel can grow in scale while its eigenvectors stay fixed. It can also
rotate, so that its eigenvectors change.

The difference matters for the accounts of the grokking time. Recent work times
grokking by scale-like parameters: the weight norm \citep{khanhWeightNormSets2026}, the scale of the
logits \citep{khanhWhatDoesWeight2026}, or the weight-decay rate
\citep{kimGrokkingWeightDecayClock2026,pracherSpectralTheoryGrokking2026}. The
result that \citet{kumarGrokkingTransitionLazy2024} cite for weight decay forcing
the kernel to change is that of \citet{lewkowyczTrainingDynamicsDeep2021}, in
which the kernel only shrinks. None of these accounts measures whether the
kernel rotates.

Rotation is important for the lazy-to-rich account of grokking. A kernel that only grows keeps its eigenvectors, so a
large distance does not by itself show that features were learned.

We ask what sets the grokking time: the shape of the kernel, its scale, or the
weight-decay rate. Our hypothesis is that rotation (change in shape) sets it. We measure three
statistics of the empirical tangent kernel along training: its scale $S_t$, its
rotation $R_t$ and its alignment $A_t$ with the labels. We do so on a grid of
720 runs that crosses laziness, width and weight decay. Our contributions are as
follows.
\begin{enumerate}[1.]
  \item We split the movement of the kernel into a change of scale and a
    rotation, and use each one as a timing variable. On the baseline of
    \citet{kumarGrokkingTransitionLazy2024}, the usual distance is mostly a
    change of scale: at $\alpha=1$ the scale accounts for \NtwoAOneGrok{} of $D_t^2$
    at grokking.
  \item We test whether grokking happens at a fixed value of each statistic,
    with a placebo test that reads each run at another run's grokking step,
    so that a statistic that merely stays in a narrow band is not mistaken for
    a threshold.
  \item We fit a power law for how the grokking time scales
    with model width and weight decay. The hypotheses that rotation sets the
    grokking time and that weight decay sets it predict different exponents.
  \item We test whether the statistics can be used to predict grokking ahead of time.
  \item We repeat the first two tests on the MNIST dataset, a task on which
    \citet{liuOmnigrokGrokkingAlgorithmic2023} induce grokking through a different method,
    to test if the findings are generalisable.
\end{enumerate}
On modular addition, rotation is the most important single statistic,
but it is not enough on its own: the grokking time depends on both width and
weight decay. While on MNIST the time depends on weight decay alone, through the
weight norm.

The works we cite each follow one quantity: the distance the kernel moves
\citep{mohamadiWhyYouGrok2024}, the weight norm \citep{khanhWeightNormSets2026},
the logit scale \citep{khanhWhatDoesWeight2026}, or the weight-decay rate
\citep{kimGrokkingWeightDecayClock2026,pracherSpectralTheoryGrokking2026}. This
report separates the growth of the tangent kernel from its rotation, and
compares on one grid the hypotheses that scale, rotation or the weight-decay
rate sets the grokking time, and then asks the same question on a second
task.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
\budget{1.2 pages}

\paragraph{Grokking.}
\citet{powerGrokkingGeneralizationOverfitting2022} report grokking on small
algorithmic datasets. \citet{nandaProgressMeasuresGrokking2023} reverse-engineer
the algorithm a small transformer learns for modular addition and split training
into memorisation, circuit formation and clean-up.
\citet{gromovGrokkingModularArithmetic2023} gives closed-form weights that solve
modular addition with a quadratic activation, and observes grokking under
full-batch gradient descent with no explicit regularisation.

\paragraph{Lazy and rich training.}
In the infinite-width limit the tangent kernel stays constant, so the network
trains as a kernel method \citep{jacotNeuralTangentKernel2020}.
\citet{chizatLazyTrainingDifferentiable2020} show that lazy training is a matter
of scale. Multiplying a model's output by a factor $\alpha$ brings its training
closer to that of its linearisation as $\alpha$ grows.
\citet{kumarGrokkingTransitionLazy2024} use this factor to move a network
between the two regimes, and propose that grokking is the transition from lazy
to rich dynamics. \citet{mohamadiWhyYouGrok2024} prove that a
permutation-equivariant kernel method needs a constant fraction of the $p^2$
pairs to learn modular addition, and observe that the kernel moves far from its
initial value only after the training data are fitted.

\paragraph{What sets the grokking time.}
\citet{khanhWeightNormSets2026} find that networks trained with weight decay
grok when the weight norm reaches a critical value, and that clamping the norm
above that value delays grokking exponentially.
\citet{khanhWhatDoesWeight2026} show that under cross-entropy the norm acts
through the scale of the logits, and that under squared error it acts through a
different route. \citet[Cor.~5, Eq.~20, arXiv v1]{kimGrokkingWeightDecayClock2026}
derives, for linear models trained with heavy-ball momentum $\beta$ and weight
decay, a grokking time of about $(1-\beta)/(\eta\lambda)$ times a logarithm of an
initial amplitude, and observes this scaling on modular addition.
\citet{pracherSpectralTheoryGrokking2026} predict, for homogeneous networks
trained on the squared error with weight decay, that the grokking time is set by
the product of the learning rate and the weight decay.
\citet{liuOmnigrokGrokkingAlgorithmic2023}
describe the same relationship in terms of the weight norm.
When weight decay is the only regulariser and the training loss is near zero
after the data are fitted, a network starting with norm $w_0$ above a critical
value $w_c$ generalises after roughly $\ln(w_0/w_c)/(\eta\lambda)$ steps.
They induce grokking on MNIST by increasing $w_0$.

\paragraph{The kernel under weight decay.}
For a network trained by gradient flow with $L_2$ strength $\lambda$, the kernel
evolves as
\begin{align}
  \frac{d\Theta_t(x,x')}{dt} &= -2(k-1)\lambda\,\Theta_t(x,x')
  + T_t(x,x') + T_t(x',x),
  \label{eq:kernelflow}\\
  T_t(x,x') &= -\sum_{x''}\partial_{\mu\nu}f_t(x)\,\partial_\mu f_t(x')\,
  \partial_\nu f_t(x'')\,\ell'(x''),\nonumber
\end{align}
where $k$ is the degree of homogeneity of the network, the sum runs over the
training inputs $x''$ and repeated parameter indices, and $\ell'$ is the
derivative of the loss with respect to the output
\citep[Eqs.~S5 and S6, arXiv v2]{lewkowyczTrainingDynamicsDeep2021}. The weight
decay appears only in the first term, which rescales the kernel. Only $T_t$ can
rotate it. At initialisation $T_0$ has expectation of order $1/N$ in the width
$N$, a result that \citet{lewkowyczTrainingDynamicsDeep2021} take from Dyer and
Gur-Ari (2020). In the infinite-width limit the kernel therefore only decays,
with fixed eigenvectors \citep[Thms.~1 and 2]{lewkowyczTrainingDynamicsDeep2021}.
\TODO{Dyer and Gur-Ari (2020) is named here only as Lewkowycz and Gur-Ari's

\paragraph{Predictions.}
\Cref{tab:predictions} sets out what each account predicts for the three tests
of this report, on either task. If rotation sets the grokking time and $T_t$ stays of order $1/N$ along
training, a wider network rotates more slowly and groks later at a fixed weight
decay. The weight decay should then matter little. A weight-decay clock (meaning that weight decay sets the grokking time) predicts
the opposite. Each account was tested on its own, so these predictions have not
been set against each other on one grid. The weight-decay accounts also leave
open what sets the time without weight decay. \Citet[p.~3, arXiv
v1]{kimGrokkingWeightDecayClock2026} lists that case, and the creation of new
directions by feature learning, among the things its mechanism does not
explain. We believe that rotation of the tangent kernel measures one form of such feature
learning.

\section{Methods}\label{sec:methods}
\budget{1.8 pages}

\subsection{Task, network and training}\label{sec:setup}

The task we train on is modular addition with modulus $p=23$, at the baseline of
\citet[App.~8.3, arXiv v3]{kumarGrokkingTransitionLazy2024}. Each pair $(a,b)$ of
residues is encoded as the concatenation $x_{a,b}\in\mathbb{R}^{2p}$ of the
one-hot codes of $a$ and $b$. Its target $y_{a,b}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}$ is the
one-hot code of $(a+b)\bmod p$. The dataset holds all $p^2=529$ pairs. Each seed
draws its own split, with 476 pairs for training and 53 for testing, and its own
initial weights.

The network is a two-layer perceptron without biases,
\begin{equation}
  f(x;\theta) = N^{-1/2}\,W^{(2)}\phi\bigl(D^{-1/2}W^{(1)}x\bigr),
  \label{eq:network}
\end{equation}
with input dimension $D=2p$, width $N$, weights $W^{(1)}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times
D}$ and $W^{(2)}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times N}$ drawn from $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$, and the
rectifier $\phi(h)=\max\{h,0\}$. The factor $N^{-1/2}$ is the NTK
parameterisation of \citet{jacotNeuralTangentKernel2020}. Following
\citet{kumarGrokkingTransitionLazy2024}, the trained predictor is
$\tilde f(x;\theta)=\alpha[f(x;\theta)-f(x;\theta_0)]$ with learning rate
$\eta=\eta_0/\alpha^2$. A larger $\alpha$ gives lazier training. The loss is the
squared error averaged over the outputs and the training pairs. Training is
full-batch gradient descent with weight decay,
$\theta_{k+1}=(1-\eta\lambda)\theta_k-\eta\nabla L(\theta_k)$. For plain
gradient descent this is the same as $L_2$ regularisation
\citep{kimGrokkingWeightDecayClock2026}. \Cref{tab:hyper} (Appendix~\ref{app:settings}) lists every setting.

\subsection{The grid}\label{sec:grid}

Each axis of the grid tests one account. The output scale $\alpha$ sets how lazy
the training is \citep{chizatLazyTrainingDifferentiable2020}. The width $N$ sets
the size of $T_t$ in \eqref{eq:kernelflow}, and so how fast the kernel can
rotate. The weight decay $\eta\lambda$ sets the clock of
\citet{kimGrokkingWeightDecayClock2026}. We call one combination of
$(\alpha,N,\eta\lambda)$ a \emph{cell}. The five runs of a cell share every
setting and differ only in their seed, which sets the split and the initial
weights. The grid has $3\times6\times8=144$ cells and 720 runs, and every run
finished.

\subsection{Scale, rotation and alignment}\label{sec:statistics}

We measure the tangent kernel of one scalar output, the sum of the logits
divided by $\sqrt p$, $g(x;\theta)=p^{-1/2}\sum_{c}f_c(x;\theta)$. This is the
pseudo-NTK of \citet{mohamadiFastWellFoundedApproximation2022}. On the 53 test
pairs of a seed, the kernel at step $t$ is the matrix with entries
$[K_t]_{ij}=\langle\nabla_\theta g(x_i;\theta_t),\nabla_\theta g(x_j;\theta_t)\rangle$.
The kernel of $\tilde f$ is $\alpha^2$ times the kernel of $f$, so the ratios
below do not depend on which of the two is used. With $Y$ the one-hot targets
of the test pairs and $C=I-\frac1{53}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top$, we record
\begin{equation}
  S_t = \frac{\Fnorm{K_t}}{\Fnorm{K_0}},\qquad
  R_t = 1-\frac{\langle K_t,K_0\rangle_F}{\Fnorm{K_t}\,\Fnorm{K_0}},\qquad
  A_t = \frac{\langle CK_tC,\,CYY^\top C\rangle_F}
  {\Fnorm{CK_tC}\,\Fnorm{CYY^\top C}}.
  \label{eq:statistics}
\end{equation}
The scale $S_t$ is 1 at initialisation, and a value above 1 means the kernel
grew. The rotation $R_t$ is the kernel distance of
\citet{fortDeepLearningKernel2020}. It is 0 at initialisation and does not
change when the kernel is rescaled. The alignment $A_t$ is the centred
alignment of \citet{cortesAlgorithmsLearningKernels2024} with the label kernel
$YY^\top$, and it also does not change when the kernel is rescaled.

The usual measure of kernel movement is the relative variation
$D_t=\Fnorm{K_t-K_0}/\Fnorm{K_0}$ \citep{geigerDisentanglingFeatureLazy2020}. It
is a function of the other two,
\begin{equation}
  D_t^2 = S_t^2 - 2S_t(1-R_t) + 1,
  \label{eq:identity}
\end{equation}
(\cref{app:identity}). A kernel that only grows has $R_t=0$ and
$D_t=\lvert S_t-1\rvert$. A large $D_t$ therefore does not show that the kernel
rotated.

\subsection{Event times}\label{sec:events}

The memorisation step of a run is the first checkpoint at which its training
accuracy reaches 0.99. Its grokking step $T$ is the first checkpoint at which
its test accuracy reaches 0.95. Appendix~\ref{app:levels} repeats the analysis
at the levels 0.80 and 0.90. Both events are counted from step 0. A run that
does not reach a level within 200\,000 steps is kept, and its grokking step is
recorded as censored at the budget (200\,000 steps). The events are read on the checkpoint grid described above,
so each one is known to lie between two checkpoints. The gap to the
checkpoint before is at most \NthreeGrok{} of the grokking step and
\NthreeMem{} of the memorisation step.

\subsection{Analysis}\label{sec:analysis}

\paragraph{Thresholds (Tier 1).} For each statistic we take its value at the
grokking step of every run that groks, and measure the spread across runs by the
standard deviation (SD). A statistic can have a small spread because it stays in
a narrow band all through training, so we include a placebo test to separate the two cases.
The placebo test reads each run at the grokking step of another run, drawn by a random
permutation with no fixed points, and measures the SD again. The tightening
ratio is the median placebo SD over 2\,000 permutations divided by the SD at the
true events, and $p$ is the share of permutations whose SD is no larger. A
ratio near 1 means the statistic is not tied to the event. The SD carries the
units of its statistic, so the SDs of different statistics cannot be compared
directly.

\paragraph{Scaling (Tier 2).} We fit the log-normal accelerated failure time
model
\begin{equation}
  \log T = c + a\log N + b\log(\eta\lambda) + \gamma\,\log N\,\log(\eta\lambda)
  + \sigma\varepsilon,\qquad \varepsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1),
  \label{eq:aft}
\end{equation}
\TODO{i think this needs more explanation for our audience}
by maximum likelihood. A censored run contributes the probability that its
grokking step exceeds the budget
\citep{kalbfleischStatisticalAnalysisFailure2002}. Both logarithms are centred at
the middle of the grid, so $a$ and $b$ are the slopes there. The fits use
lifelines \citep{davidson-pilonLifelinesSurvivalAnalysis2019}. The intervals
resample whole cells and then seeds within cells, so that they include the
misfit of the cells about the fitted plane.

\paragraph{Prediction (Tier 3).} We read $S_t$, $\log R_t$ and $A_t$ at
$\tstar=2\,430$, a saved step before any run reaches the grokking level, and
ask how well they predict the grokking step. The five seeds of a cell are close
copies, so whole groups of runs are always held out. Two tests are used. The
first holds out one cell at a time, which tests interpolation. Kernel ridge
regression with a Gaussian kernel is fitted on each subset of the statistics,
which shows which statistics carry the information. The second holds out one
value of $\alpha$ at a time, which tests extrapolation to an unseen laziness.
It compares a linear accelerated failure time model on each statistic alone,
gradient-boosted trees with the accelerated failure time objective
\citep{chenXGBoostScalableTree2016} on all three, and a gated recurrent unit
\citep{choLearningPhraseRepresentations2014} that reads all three at the 25
saved steps up to $\tstar$. The weight decay does not enter as a predictor,
since it is a setting and not a measurement (\cref{sec:tier3}). As a reference,
the second test also fits the trees to the settings $N$ and $\eta\lambda$
together and to each one alone. Two scores are used. $R^2$ is the share of the
spread in $\log T$ explained on held-out runs. The concordance index is the share
of pairs of runs that a model puts in the right order, so that 0.5 is a coin toss
\citep{harrellEvaluatingYieldMedical1982}. Appendix~\ref{app:fitting} gives the
settings of each model.

\subsection{The second task}\label{sec:mnistsetup}

In tier 4 we apply the same questions from Tiers 1 and 2 to the
MNIST dataset using the same setup with which \citet{liuOmnigrokGrokkingAlgorithmic2023}
induce grokking. The network is trained on a subset of $1000$ images and the initial weights
are multiplied by a scale $s>1$ so the weight decay must shrink the norm before the network generalises.
This differs from \cref{sec:setup} which subtracts $f(x;\theta_0)$ so that every run is centered
and starts from the zero function. Centring eliminates grokking delay on MNIST and
instead the train and test accuracies rise at relatively the same time which is why it cannot be used.
The architecture is a bias-free three-layer rectifier network of width $N$
in the standard parameterisation, trained by minibatch gradient descent without momentum,
with the learning rate reset from the curvature every $10000$ steps.
The values tested on the grid are $s\in\{3,5,7,9\}$, $N\in\{100,200,400,800\}$ and
seven decays from 0 to $4\times10^{-5}$, each with 5 seeds.
Each run has a budget of $500000$ steps and the grokking level is $0.80$
since no run reaches 0.95 (Appendix~\ref{app:mnist}).

\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Baseline: the reproduction of Kumar et al.}\label{sec:baseline}
\budget{0.8 page}

\textbf{Finding.} Our runs reproduce the baseline of
\citet{kumarGrokkingTransitionLazy2024}. The network memorises within a few
thousand steps and groks much later, and a lazier network groks later. Over this
time the kernel grows several-fold while $R_t$ stays below 0.09.
% 0.09 bounds the largest R_sum over every checkpoint of the 15 baseline runs

\subsection{Tier 1: is there a threshold?}\label{sec:tier1}

grokking step, but reaching that rotation does not make a network grok. The
alignment at grokking barely differs from its value at initialisation. Across
widths, no statistic takes a fixed value at grokking.

width sets the rate at which the kernel rotates. \Cref{sec:tier2} tests this.

and that does not hold. Other claims of a fixed value at grokking, such as the
critical weight norm of \citet{khanhWeightNormSets2026}, can be given the same
placebo check.

\subsection{Tier 2: width or weight decay?}\label{sec:tier2}
\budget{2.0 pages}

\textbf{Finding.} The grokking time depends on both width and weight decay, so
neither the rotation clock nor the weight-decay clock of
\cref{tab:predictions} holds on its own. Width sets the rotation of the kernel.
At a fixed width, weight decay shrinks its scale.

Without weight decay, only $N=50$ and $N=100$ grok within the budget
(\cref{fig:map}). The wider networks memorise and stop there. From
$\eta\lambda=10^{-5}$ to $10^{-4}$ every seed at every width groks, and the time
falls with $\eta\lambda$ at every width. The largest weight decay at which every

$N=800$ and $N=1600$. At $10^{-3}$ and above no width groks. A wider network
thus groks later at the same weight decay and stops grokking at a smaller one,

\subsection{Tier 3: predicting the grokking time from the kernel}\label{sec:tier3}
\budget{1.6 pages}

\textbf{Finding.} Read before any run groks, rotation is the best single predictor
of the grokking time on held-out cells. Scale adds information that rotation lacks, and alignment
adds little. On an unseen $\alpha$, the three statistics together rank runs
better than any one of them alone. The settings $N$ and $\eta\lambda$ rank them
better still, and better than either setting alone, which supports the mixed
clock of \cref{sec:tier2}.

Each account in \cref{tab:predictions} names a different early predictor:
$R_t$ for the rotation clock, $S_t$ for the norm or scale clock, and
$\eta\lambda$ alone for the weight-decay clock. This tier tests the first
two, with $A_t$ beside them. It does not test $\eta\lambda$, which we set and
know exactly, while a general network reports neither its effective
regularisation nor its laziness. Its tangent kernel can be measured. We want
predictors that carry over to real models, so this tier asks how much of the
grokking time the kernel statistics recover on their own. A model given the
settings $N$ and $\eta\lambda$ serves as a reference that knows more than a
general network would report.

\subsection{Tier 4: a second task, MNIST digits}\label{sec:tier4mnist}
\budget{1.0 page}

\textbf{Finding.} On MNIST the weight-decay clock of \cref{tab:predictions}
holds exactly. The grokking time falls inversely proportional to weight decay
and does not depend on width. At grokking, the weight norm reaches a fixed value,
and the kernel shrinks with it while its shape remains nearly unchanged.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.86\linewidth]{figures/E19_mnist.pdf}
  \caption{MNIST at $s=7$. Panels (a) and (b) use widths 200 to 800;
    (c) adds width 100, which reaches the level later and off the law
    (Appendix~\ref{app:mnist}). (a) The grokking step against
    weight decay, medians over 5 seeds, coloured by the initial scale $s$ with
    one marker per width. The lines are $T=\ln(s/n_c)/(\eta\lambda)$ with the
    mean $n_c$ of panel (b), and the dotted line is the budget. (b) The weight
    norm at grokking in units of the norm at $s=1$, one point per run, with its
    mean. (c) The map of \cref{fig:map} at $s=7$: the median grokking step
    over the seeds where at least 3 of 5 grok, the seeds that grok out of 5,
    and the phase letter of \cref{fig:map} where none does.}
  \label{fig:mnist}
\end{figure}

In the main grid range, $s=7$ at $N\in\{200,400,800\}$ with $\eta\lambda$
from $3\times10^{-6}$ to $10^{-5}$, every seed groks, and the fit of
\eqref{eq:aft} gives $a=\EnineteenMainA$ [\EnineteenMainALo,
\EnineteenMainAHi] and $b=\EnineteenMainB$ [\EnineteenMainBLo,
\EnineteenMainBHi], against \EsixMainA{} and \EsixMainB{} on modular addition
(\cref{tab:scaling}), with a residual $\sigma$ of \EnineteenMainS{} against
\EsixMainS{}.

This is likely a direct result of the weight norm. The product $\eta\lambda T$ is constant
within each scale and increases with $s$ (\cref{fig:mnist}a), and at grokking the
norm is \EnineteenNc{} times the norm at $s=1$, with a standard deviation of
\EnineteenNcSd{} (\EnineteenNcRel) over the \EnineteenNcRuns{} runs that
reach the level, across every scale, width and decay (\cref{fig:mnist}b). As in \citet{liuOmnigrokGrokkingAlgorithmic2023} the
norm follows pure decay and the time is given by the law $T=\ln(s/n_c)/(\eta\lambda)$
(Appendix~\ref{app:mnist}). As the norm falls, the kernel shrinks too where at grokking,
it is between \EnineteenSWTwohundred{} and \EnineteenSWEighthundred{} of its initial value.
As in modular addition the rotation decreases with width but in MNIST falls sharply
going from \EnineteenRWTwohundred{} at $N=200$ to \EnineteenRWEighthundred{}
at $N=800$ while $T$ remains approximately the same. The Tier 1 placebo test cannot
distinguish these statistics here (Appendix~\ref{app:mnist}).

The two tasks therefore grok by different routes. Grokking in MNIST is set by the
\citet{liuOmnigrokGrokkingAlgorithmic2023} problem construction where the
enlarged initial function must decay and the kernel need only shrink before generalisation occurs. On
modular addition the delay happens because of the centring, and
\citet{mohamadiWhyYouGrok2024} prove that a fixed permutation-equivariant
kernel cannot learn the task from a constant fraction of the pairs. The
kernel must change shape first and does so at a rate that depends on width.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}
\budget{0.6 page}

Every modular addition run uses the NTK parameterisation, one task and
one architecture. A width effect measured here belongs to this
parameterisation. Each cell has 5 seeds. The events are known only to the
resolution of the checkpoint grid. The fits in Tier 2 depend on which cells
enter, and the decay edge bends the plane (\cref{tab:scaling}). The kernel
statistics say nothing about which seed of a cell groks first, and the
reference model given the settings predicts better than any kernel model. The
alignment here uses the kernel of the summed logits. The tangent kernel of all
$p$ logits may behave differently. The centred predictor is not homogeneous,
so the theorem behind \eqref{eq:kernelflow} applies to our network only as a
guide. The proposal also planned a clamped-norm arm, a sweep over $p$ and the
training fraction, and a cross-entropy arm. They were not run. The proposal
defined the events by the loss. The report uses accuracy (\cref{sec:events}).

The MNIST runs differ in not only the task but the architecture and
method for inducing grokking, with the most impactful of these being the
uncentred predictor. Our comparison assumes that none of these changes
which clock applies. With only two tasks to compare we cannot tell which of them,
if any, is responsible for the different result. Tier 4 tests which clock
sets the time once a delay exists, not whether rotation can cause one.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
\budget{0.3 page}

We asked what sets the grokking time: the shape of the tangent kernel, its
scale, or the weight-decay rate. On modular addition, rotation matters
most and is not enough on its own. At a fixed width it is the statistic most
tied to the grokking step, yet networks can reach that rotation without
grokking. The grokking time grows with width and falls with weight decay.
Width sets the rotation, and at a fixed width weight decay shrinks the scale.
Early rotation and scale together predict the grokking time better than either
one alone, and width and weight decay together predict it better than either
setting alone. On MNIST, \citet{liuOmnigrokGrokkingAlgorithmic2023} create
a delay by increasing the initial function's scale. The weight-decay clock predicts
the grokking time exactly using the law \(\ln(s/n_c)/(\eta\lambda)\), with no
dependence on width. The kernel shrinks but rotates very little where width
affects only the rotation. Weight decay therefore sets the grokking time through
the weight norm on both tasks, and width enters only on modular addition,
where a fixed kernel cannot solve the task and the kernel must change shape first.
What is new is the split into scale and rotation, the placebo test, the crossed grid on two
tasks, and prediction from quantities that any network exposes. A next step
is to test whether the early rate of rotation falls as $1/N$, and
whether a kernel frozen at each step can generalise on either task.

\section{Settings of the prediction models}\label{app:fitting}
\budget{1.0 page}

All models use the 630 runs with $\eta\lambda\le10^{-3}$, with $S_t$,
$\log R_t$ and $A_t$ read at $\tstar$. The grokking step of a censored run is
known only to exceed 200\,000. Whole groups are held out: one value of $\alpha$
at a time (\cref{fig:ladder}) or one cell at a time (\cref{tab:subsets}). The
file \file{report_v2/code/models.py} fixes every setting below.

\paragraph{Linear models.} The log-normal accelerated failure time model of a
single statistic standardises it on the training fold and uses a ridge penalty
of 0.01.

\paragraph{Kernel ridge.} The kernel is Gaussian on standardised inputs. Its
penalty in $\{0.01,0.1,1\}$ and its width in $\{0.1,0.5,2\}$ are chosen by grouped
5-fold cross-validation over the training cells, inside every outer fold. It is
fitted to $\log T$ on the training runs that grok.

\paragraph{Gradient-boosted trees.} The trees use the accelerated failure time
objective with normal errors of scale 1 and a subsample of 0.8. The search covers
depths 1 to 4, learning rates 0.03 and 0.1, and minimum child weights 1, 5 and 10,
with up to 1\,000 rounds and early stopping after 50. The setting with the best
concordance on 20\% of the training cells is refitted on all training runs. The
seeds are 0 to 4. The reference model is the same search on $\log N$ and
$\log_{10}(\eta\lambda)$, with $\eta\lambda=0$ set to $10^{-6}$. Its two parts are
the same search on $\log N$ alone and on $\log_{10}(\eta\lambda)$ alone.

\paragraph{Recurrent network.} One gated recurrent layer with 16 units reads
$A_t$, $S_t$ and $R_t$ at the 25 saved steps up to $\tstar$. $R_t$ enters
without a logarithm because it is 0 at step 0. The inputs are standardised on
the training fold. A linear head gives the location and a learned parameter the
scale of a log-normal accelerated failure time loss. Training uses Adam with
learning rate 0.01 and weight decay $10^{-4}$, on the full batch, for up to 800
epochs. Every 10 epochs the loss on 20\% of the training cells is checked, and the
best weights are kept. Early stopping chose between \EfifteenGruEpochsMin{} and
\EfifteenGruEpochsMax{} epochs. The seeds are 0 to 2.
